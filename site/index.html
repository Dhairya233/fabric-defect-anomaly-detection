Strong on a leaky split, near chance on an unseen fabric

Texture features and anomaly detectors were scored on 245 fabric strips three different ways. For the supervised models, how the data is split changes the answer more than which model is used.

Precomputed on held-out images; not a live model.

    Method sheet

    All learned methods use the same 90 texture features per 256×256 patch: grey-level co-occurrence statistics, local binary patterns, Gabor filter responses, Fourier band energies and intensity moments. Each strip is first normalised to a robust z-score so that features describe texture rather than the brightness of one fabric.

    Raw contrastBaseline

    Score is the spread of raw pixel values in the patch. No training.

    Why it is here. A one-line rule any real method must beat.

    Weakness. It mostly measures which fabric it is looking at, and whether a blank margin is in view.

    Brightness deviationBaseline

    Score is how far the patch’s average brightness is from the average clean patch.

    Weakness. It separates clean from defective strips in some fabrics because of how they were captured, not because of defects (see About the data).

    Random forestSupervised

    300 trees on the texture features, trained on labelled patches (defective or clean) from the training strips.

    Weakness. It learns the fabrics it was shown. On a new fabric its score drops to about chance, and its patch-level scores inflate when patches leak across the split.

    SVM (RBF)Supervised

    An RBF-kernel support vector machine on the same features. The penalty C is the only setting tuned, over three values, on validation patches only.

    Weakness. Same as the random forest.

    Mahalanobis, globalAnomaly

    Learns what normal fabric looks like from defect-free strips of every training fabric (mean and a shrinkage covariance of the features). A patch is scored by its distance from normal.

    Weakness. One description of normal for several fabrics blurs them, so a different fabric can look like a defect.

    Mahalanobis, per fabricAnomaly

    The same idea with a separate model for each fabric. It needs to be told which fabric it is looking at, so it cannot run on a fabric it never trained on.

    Behaviour. It raised no false alarms on the 140 clean strips in the grouped split, and found fewer than half of the defective ones.

    Isolation forestAnomaly

    Scores a patch by how easily random splits isolate it from defect-free patches.

    Weakness. Scored below chance on unseen fabrics, and on one fabric even within the grouped split. I have not found the cause.

    One-class SVMAnomaly

    Learns a boundary around defect-free patches (RBF kernel, nu = 0.05) and scores distance outside it.

    Weakness. Close to chance on the three cleanest fabrics.

    Cost on one CPU core: extracting features takes about 3 seconds per strip and dominates everything. Scoring a strip afterwards takes between 0.1 and 5 milliseconds. These are timings from a sandbox machine, not a deployment benchmark.

    Limits

    • Small dataset. 245 strips from 7 fabrics, 105 of them defective. One fabric has a single defective strip, and nine of twelve defect types have fewer than ten examples, so per-type numbers are not reliable.
    • One public benchmark. Nothing here says how these methods behave on another dataset.
    • Not tested on real factory footage. The images are lab captures of cut strips, not a moving line under production lighting, and nothing here measures speed on a real line.
    • Intervals are narrower than the full uncertainty. They reflect which test strips were drawn, not a different random assignment of strips to folds (which moves the random forest by about ±0.02 and the SVM by ±0.05). Claims about new fabrics rest on six or seven fabrics: resampling fabrics widens the random forest’s unseen-fabric interval to 0.34 to 0.72.
    • Some analysis choices came after seeing results. The margin-masked run, the three-fabric subset and the preference for per-fabric AUROC were decided after looking at the data, which is why both runs are shown.
    • Localisation is coarse by design. Scores exist per 256-pixel patch, stepped every 128 pixels, while the median defect covers about 280 pixels. No method locates a defect precisely.
    • No deep features. Pretrained-network methods are the most important missing comparison. I could not download pretrained weights in the environment used.
    • Capture artefacts remain. White margins were left out, but dark edge bands and clean-versus-defective brightness differences cannot all be removed.

    About the data

    Looking at failure cases showed that the dataset itself can flatter a model. 204 of 245 strips have blank white margins where the camera sees past the fabric, and the amount of margin differs between clean and defective strips of the same fabric.

    Clean and defective strips are not always captured alike

    A defect covers about 0.03% of a strip, so it cannot change the strip’s average brightness. Where brightness alone separates the classes, the strips were captured under different conditions.

    Strip AUROC on the three fabrics where they look alike

    Fabrics 03, 04 and 06 show no brightness difference, so they are the fairest test. The rule that picked them was written after looking at the numbers, so read this as a sensitivity check, not a pre-planned test. Margins left out; mean of per-fabric AUROC, 47 defective strips.
